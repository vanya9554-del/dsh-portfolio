1The test that matters: calibration

If a market is efficient, contracts priced at 0.30 should resolve YES 30% of the time. Plot realised frequency against price and an efficient market lies on the diagonal. Below: 7-day and 30-day buckets, bubble size proportional to sample count.

At 7 days the dots sit above the diagonal in the middle and upper buckets — prices understate the true probability. At 30 days the pattern is weaker but still tilted upward. This is the classic favourite–longshot pattern, and it is exactly what fee-free arbitrage would erase; 5–7% Jupiter fees are what keep it alive.

2Bias by horizon, with t-statistics

Bias in percentage points between realised frequency and implied price, all buckets. The 7-day horizon is the only one that survives a two-sided test at t = 4.834563560438896, and it is the horizon a small account can actually trade: long enough to have edge, short enough to recycle capital.

5Method & data

  • Universe: every Polymarket market resolvable within 180 days, priced through the Jupiter prediction API; sports and up/down markets excluded; liquidity and spread filters applied.
  • Calibration: 12 price buckets per horizon (1, 7, 30, 90, 180 days), binomial realised frequency, Wilson intervals, two-sided t-tests of bias vs zero.
  • Fees: Jupiter contract fee sampled per market (4–7% on cheap contracts), included in net EV.
  • Pipeline: fetch → match → calibrate → backtest, all in the public repository.
